## What changed

OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC and NCUA propose new third-party risk management guidance (comments due November 16, 2026) that, if finalized, would replace the 2023 Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships (88 FR 37920) and the supplemental resources (OCC Bulletin 2002-16, the 2024 bank-fintech deposit statement, and the 2024 community bank guide). It reorganizes TPRM into four components (sec. B): risk identification and assessment (relationship inventory, risk identification, risk assessment); risk oversight (due diligence and selection, contract negotiation, ongoing monitoring, termination); residual risk acceptance; and governance. It extends to insured credit unions, tailors oversight to assessed risk rather than 'critical activities', defines a third-party relationship as a business arrangement typically under a written agreement, says subcontractors alone do not create a direct oversight presumption, states the guidance is non-enforceable and that deviation alone will not be a basis for supervisory action, and notes consumer compliance considerations are not directly addressed.

## What a compliant agent must now do

No change until final. If finalized, bank and credit union clients would scale due diligence, contract terms and ongoing monitoring of an AI servicing provider to their own risk assessment of that relationship; a servicing agent that talks to borrowers, handles payments or data is likely to be assessed as higher risk. The provider should be ready to supply evidence proportionate to that assessment (controls, incident history, compliance testing, subcontractor list), and register row TPRM-001 would need its citation updated once the 2023 guidance is rescinded.
