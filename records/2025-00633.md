## What changed

CFPB proposes new 12 CFR part 1027 (Regulation AA) under 12 U.S.C. 5531(b). Subpart B codifies the FTC Credit Practices Rule for all CFPA covered persons: 1027.202 makes it unfair to enter into or enforce contracts with confessions of judgment, waivers of exemption, wage assignments (with exceptions for revocable assignments and payroll-deduction payment plans) or non-purchase-money security interests in household goods; 1027.203 makes it deceptive to misrepresent cosigner liability and unfair to obligate a cosigner without the prescribed 'Notice to Cosigner'; 1027.204 makes it unfair, in collecting a consumer credit debt, to levy or collect a delinquency charge on an otherwise full, on-time payment when the only delinquency is from late fees on earlier installments (late-fee pyramiding). Subpart C (1027.301) would bar terms purporting to waive substantive legal rights, reserving unilateral amendment of material terms, or restraining lawful free expression. Proposed compliance date 30 days after the final rule (180 days for existing agreements). Comments were due April 1, 2025. Withdrawn May 15, 2025 (2025-08645).

## What a compliant agent must now do

No change until final, and the proposal was withdrawn on May 15, 2025. Had it been adopted: servicers and collectors could not assess late fees pyramided on prior late fees (1027.204), could not misrepresent a cosigner's liability in collection contacts (1027.203), and could not enforce prohibited contract provisions such as wage assignments or waivers of rights. The FTC Credit Practices Rule itself (16 CFR part 444) continues to apply to creditors under FTC jurisdiction regardless of this withdrawal.
