## What changed

CFPB amends Regulation V subpart D: adds 12 CFR 1022.3(j) defining 'medical debt information' (debt owed to a health care provider or its agent or assignee, including bills not past due or paid); removes and reserves the financial information exception in 1022.30(d), so creditors may no longer obtain or use medical debt information in any determination of eligibility or continued eligibility for credit unless a 1022.30(e) exception applies; revises the unsolicited-information rule in 1022.30(c) so unsolicited medical information may be used only under an (e) exception (examples: a borrower mentions a hospital debt or a medical condition to a loan officer); revises (e)(1)(viii)-(ix) (debt cancellation, debt suspension and credit insurance triggered by a medical event) and adds (e)(1)(x) (medical information in account transaction data or relating to income, benefits or loan purpose, used no less favorably than comparable non-medical information); adds 1022.38 limiting CRAs from furnishing medical debt information to creditors. Applies to creditors other than dealers excluded by CFPA section 1029. Effective March 17, 2025 (the printed 'March 17, 2024' was corrected by C1-2024-30824).

## What a compliant agent must now do

Creditors (lenders and servicers deciding on continued eligibility), from March 17, 2025: when an agent evaluates an extension, modification or other continued-eligibility decision, do not request or use a borrower's medical debt information or medical condition except under a 1022.30(e) exception; if a borrower volunteers medical information in conversation, it may be used only under an (e) exception (for example, triggering debt cancellation, debt suspension or credit insurance benefits under (e)(1)(viii)-(ix)), and the agent must not factor the borrower's health, condition or treatment into the decision.
