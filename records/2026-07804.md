## What changed

CFPB final rule, effective July 21, 2026, adopting its November 2025 proposal as proposed: revised 12 CFR 1002.6(a) states 'The Act does not provide that the "effects test" applies for determining whether there is discrimination in violation of the Act', and new comment 6(a)-2 limits prohibited practices to disparate treatment, reaching facially neutral criteria only when they function as proxies designed or applied with intent to advantage or disadvantage on a protected basis; revised 1002.4(b) and comment 4(b)-1 limit discouragement to oral or written statements directed at applicants or prospective applicants that a reasonable person would read as signalling denial or worse terms because of a prohibited basis (interview scripts that discourage on a prohibited basis remain prohibited); revised 1002.8(a)(3), (b)(2)-(4) and (c) add conditions for for-profit special purpose credit programs and bar race, color, national origin or sex as eligibility criteria; 1002.15(d)(1)(ii) revised.

## What a compliant agent must now do

From July 21, 2026, creditors (including in collection and servicing, which ECOA covers as 'any aspect of a credit transaction'): continue to prohibit any difference in treatment on a prohibited basis in contact, hardship, settlement and modification decisions, and never use prohibited-basis characteristics or intentional proxies for them. Under Regulation B as amended, outcome disparities from neutral servicing policies are no longer a Regulation B violation by themselves; do not remove disparity monitoring without counsel review because state law and other enforcers may differ.
