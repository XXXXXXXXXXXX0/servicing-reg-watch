## What changed

FCC announces April 11, 2025 as the effective date of the TCPA consent-revocation rules in FCC 24-24 (89 FR 15756), after OMB approved the information collections on September 26, 2024. Per the eCFR diff: new 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10) lets a called party revoke prior express consent (including prior express written consent) for calls and texts under 64.1200(a)(1)-(3) and (c)(2) by any reasonable method; an automated voice or key-press opt-out, a reply text of 'stop', 'quit', 'end', 'revoke', 'opt out', 'cancel' or 'unsubscribe', or a caller-designated website or number is per se reasonable; other reply wording counts if a reasonable person would read it as revocation; one-way text protocols must disclose that and give alternative opt-out means on each text; revocations must be honored within a reasonable time not exceeding ten business days; callers may not designate an exclusive revocation channel. New 64.1200(a)(11): other means (e.g., voicemail or email) create a rebuttable presumption of revocation. 64.1200(a)(9)(i)(F) (package delivery opt-outs) moves from 30 days to six business days and 64.1200(d)(3) (do-not-call requests) from 30 days to ten business days.

## What a compliant agent must now do

From April 11, 2025, any caller or texter using an autodialer or an artificial or prerecorded voice (including an AI voice agent), first-party or third-party: (1) treat any reasonable expression of 'don't contact me' as a revocation, including the listed keywords and free-text replies a reasonable person would read as revocation, spoken requests during calls, and requests by voicemail or email (rebuttable presumption); (2) stop the robocalls/robotexts covered by 64.1200(a)(1)-(3) and (c)(2) within ten business days of receipt at most; (3) never require a specific channel to revoke; (4) if texts are sent on a no-reply protocol, state that on each text and give alternative opt-out means; (5) send at most one confirmation text, without marketing, preferably within five minutes (64.1200(a)(12)).
