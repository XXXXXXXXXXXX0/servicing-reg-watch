## What changed

CFPB Supervisory Highlights Issue 36 (student lending; exams generally completed in 2024) reports UDAAP findings (12 U.S.C. 5531, 5536) and Regulation Z violations. Servicing-relevant findings: sec. 2.1.3 failing to re-amortize or offer to re-amortize a consolidated loan after removing loans the borrower excluded (unfair); sec. 2.2.1 denying total-and-permanent-disability discharges on criteria not in the loan note (unfair); sec. 2.2.2 telling borrowers on portals they were ineligible for an autopay rate discount when they were eligible (deceptive); sec. 2.2.3 advertising payment suspension for unemployment after replacing it with a narrower program (deceptive, abusive); sec. 2.3.1-2.3.3 servicers telling borrowers with holder-clause notes (16 CFR 433.2) that no discharge was available and refusing to consider school-misconduct claims unless the borrower had an attorney, with direction to run an accessible claims-review process and suspend collections in some cases; sec. 2.4.1 collection letters implying legal action the servicer never took (deceptive); sec. 2.4.2 model contracts allowing transcript withholding on default (risk of abusive practice). The introduction also reports federal loan servicing violations on return to repayment (communication channels, deceptive billing statements, excess withdrawals from deposit accounts, IDR processing), in a part not read. Refinance-marketing findings (2.1.1, 2.1.2, 2.1.4 under 12 CFR 1026.47(c)/1026.48(d)) concern origination.

## What a compliant agent must now do

Consumer-loan servicers and their agents, as UDAAP supervisory expectations (student loans directly; the same reasoning applies to other consumer loans): (1) describe hardship, deferment, discharge and discount options exactly as the loan contract and current policy provide, and apply only the contract's eligibility criteria; (2) never imply legal action, fees or consequences the servicer does not actually pursue; (3) when a borrower raises a claim or defense the contract allows (e.g., a holder-in-due-course clause), take it into a documented review without requiring an attorney, and do not say no remedy exists; (4) when a loan is modified or reduced, offer the borrower the resulting payment options (e.g., re-amortization) rather than silently shortening the term; (5) withdraw only authorized amounts from borrowers' accounts.
